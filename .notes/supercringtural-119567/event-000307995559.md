Supernatural

By Barry Garron

Bottom line: Why wait for Halloween? There's lots of scary spooks and thrills right here, right now.
9-10 p.m.
Tuesday, Sept. 13
WB Network

It has been described as a cross between "Route 66" and "The X-Files." As descriptions go, that's not bad, but "Supernatural" is too complex and robust to be so easily categorized. It is as close as TV has come to importing the kind of horror associated with theatricals, but it combines those spine-tingling moments with camaraderie and mystery. Plus, it explores some intriguing and popular ghostly legends.

Jared Padalecki and Jensen Ackles, alumni of successful youth-oriented fare on WB Network (Padalecki from "Gilmore Girls," Ackles from "Smallville"), co-star as Sam and Dean Winchester, brothers with an eerie family history. When they were small children, their mother perished in an incident that defied both the laws of gravity and combustion. Their father raised the boys to track down spooks as he devoted his life to finding out who or what took his wife. Sam, tiring of the chase, tried to pursue a more normal lifestyle, but it wasn't to be. In the opener, Dean persuades Sam to join him in a search for their father, who dropped out of sight during a recent spirit hunt.

Sam is the voice of restraint and conscience, while Dean is more impetuous, a free spirit and risk taker. (Their names are an homage to Sal and Dean in Jack Kerouac's "On the Road," a favorite with creator Eric Kripke.) In the 70-minute premiere, they confront a woman in white with a penchant for hitchhiking on dark highways, always to the detriment of the male driver.

It's hard to know what to pack for such occasions. Sam and Dean keep enough weaponry in the trunk of their 1967 Chevrolet Impala to host a small gun and firearms show. They lack only business cards that say, "Hate Ghosts, Will Travel."

David Nutter, a veteran "X-Files" director and the go-to helmsman for many WB pilots ("Jack & Bobby," "Tarzan," "Smallville," "Roswell"), directed the pilot and dotted it with several edge-of-your-seat moments. Equally important, he blended these moments with other strong dramatic elements, including the reconciliation of the brothers and their constant struggles to investigate the paranormal while avoiding suspicious but invariably dull-witted local authorities. Maintaining that pace and tone, as well as some glorious special effects, will be a challenge every bit as great as defeating the weekly demonic forces.

The Tuesday night competition includes "House" on Fox, "The Amazing Race" on CBS and promising newcomers on NBC ("My Name Is Earl") and ABC ("Commander in Chief"). Still, "Supernatural" is unlike anything else out there and should build a following among viewers who appreciate its combination of spooky mythology and mystery adventure.
